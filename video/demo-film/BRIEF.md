A 3:00, 16:9 (1920×1080) product film. Built in Remotion. Two worlds:

- **The past** (0:00–0:46): black-and-white archival film: grain, soft bloom, gate weave. Kinetic type is bold Helvetica with italic Didot accents, plus one orange accent (`#ff5a1f`).
- **The product** (0:46–2:38): a hard cut into crisp, full-colour TYSA. The real app, freshly captured at 2× resolution, flown through in 3D: the camera dives into the probe card, the plan graph draws itself, the thinking field types live, and the knowledge graph spreads out like a starfield.

## 2. B-roll shots for you to source

Ten shots, all for the archival half and the close. Rules for every shot:

- **16:9, at least 1920×1080, 24 fps, 5 s** (I trim each to 3–4 s).
- **No text, logos, watermarks or captions** in the picture. Keep the empty side clear; type goes there.
- **Light film grain at most.** I add grain, bloom, weave and the black-and-white grade in post, so every shot matches.
- **One camera move per shot**, as written.
- For the **history shots (S04–S06)**, real public-domain film is better than AI. Generated footage must never pass as historical record. Public-domain options are listed under each one. Check each item's licence page before you download.

Prompts are written for Seedance 2.5 / Kling (Higgsfield or similar). Paste them as they are.

## 4. Product capture (Claude does this)

A fresh lesson captured at 2× resolution (3840×2160) so close-ups stay sharp.

**Lesson topic: "Why does my phone's GPS need Einstein's relativity to work?"**
It's concrete and surprising (uncorrected, satellite clocks drift ~38 µs a day, and positions drift ~10 km a day). It also needs a real dependency chain (signal timing → trilateration → clock error → special and general relativity), and it makes the app show off: a research sub-agent checks the 38 µs figure, a precise SVG figure for trilateration, and an interactive demo where you drag a clock-error slider and watch your position drift.

The learner run is scripted: one confident wrong probe answer (so the probe visibly adapts), messy thinking typed into the thinking field, a correct prediction, one practice stall that gets a minimal hint, and the knowledge graph at the end.

Other topics, if you'd rather:
- **The hairy ball theorem** (why there is always a windless spot on Earth): the most visual, more abstract.
- **How public-key cryptography lets strangers share a secret in public**: great "aha" moments, less visual.
